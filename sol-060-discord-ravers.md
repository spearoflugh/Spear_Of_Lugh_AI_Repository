# Discord Ravers

Off the top of your head, and without using a search engine, can you name a single author of the
IPCC report? Global warming — or "climate change," a less dangerous formulation avoiding falsification
— is supposedly the defining question of our time. You can't. Scientific expeditions and the cultural
reception of science have dramatically changed. This is another symptom of the digital revolution.

## Just Another Data Point in the Wall

Our modern era is the direct descendant of explorers and their expeditions. The scientific expedition
is a product of the Enlightenment: this is when the "Explorer" — notebook in hand, measuring the
stars — was born. The Enlightenment introduced the idea that knowledge itself was a treasure worth
risking one's life for.

Fast forward to the 21st century. Anyone can explore the globe at the swipe of a finger using
Google Earth. Knowledge has become a commodity — now called "data" — and the only science that
remains is statistics. The "Eureka" moment has vanished from the cultural landscape. The unknown
no longer teases; it irritates. We have become like customers in a fast-food restaurant, impatiently
waiting for our order to be served.

The spirit of discovery has been gamified, as more and more discoveries are now made by crunching
data and experienced through screens. Since data is churned out by a machine, an absence of data
is viewed as a flat tire — a mere technical glitch. There is indeed this "1975 test": if you were
to enter a room in 1975 and then in 2025, except for the screens and digital devices, hardly any
difference would be noted. However, if you transported a witness from 1925 to 1975, the story would
be completely different. From exploring the universe to debugging a flawed app. Talk about a
fulfilling evolution.

## Relativity

The focus of online conversation moves fast. It contrasts sharply with the lifelong obsessions of
past adventurers. Instead of digging deep into a question, the crux of the fight is now to surface
on the "discussion du jour" ahead of everyone else.

Take the war against Iran. The goal of the war shifts on a daily basis. At the start of the conflict,
Trump wasn't saying a word about the Strait of Hormuz; now, there are multiple daily statements:
"we will open it," "it is open," "we will close it," "we need help from allies," "our military is
the best and can open it by itself."

All scales are mixed: what happens today, what happened yesterday, and what might happen a few weeks
from now or indefinitely. Notice how official statements jump from one time horizon to another. The
main objective is to escape accountability. Democracy cannot function without the exploration of
ideas, which is rendered impossible by these tactics.

## The Gloomy Spirit

Spiritus in Latin means wind, itself from spirare, to breathe. This is the wind that allows the
explorers — whether in real life or in the world of ideas — to navigate to new shores. In ancient
times it was more or less synonymous with life: you were declared dead if you stopped breathing.
Today you are declared dead if your ECG is a flat line. The proof of life has moved from the
objective world to the world of ideas.

In the world of ideas there is no physical wind nor feelings, no physical needs helping explorers
to orient themselves. The role is filled by faith. And if there is another certainty of our times,
it is that the most rapidly growing religion is atheism. The global fertility rate is another
second-hand symptom.

On the networks, information moves at the speed of light, but humans are stalled, lacking intent
and hope. If you don't move, or run in circles, it is functionally equivalent to being in jail.
Yet the landscape surrounding you is free from obstacles. Maybe this is the reason why the zeitgeist
is so frantic. This paradox has yet to be cracked.
