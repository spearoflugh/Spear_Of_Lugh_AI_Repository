# A Maze Inc.

Palantir has been making the rounds on social media following the publication of its manifesto.
This move by a major player has paved the way for other corporations to publish their own versions.
Nym positions itself in polar opposition to Palantir despite acting the same — both have diagnosed
that old forms of power are reaching a point of obsolescence. That they share this view is not
surprising; what is more interesting is that they now feel the time is ripe to go public with it.

This shift marks a new phase: Deep Tech is openly challenging the Deep State's legitimacy. According
to Max Weber, the modern state is defined by its monopoly on the legitimate use of physical force.
Both manifestos touch on areas — weaponry, cryptographic tools, the organization of power — that
sit at the very heart of the nation-state.

## What is Going On?

Since the start of the second Trump administration, the most significant political opposition is
between the Deep State and Deep Tech. The powers that be are increasingly resembling distributed
systems rather than vertical administrations.

Consider the language shift in corporate-state relations. In 2018, the headline read: "Amazon's
Bid on a $10 Billion Pentagon Contract Is Riddled With Conflicts of Interest." In 2025: "Amazon
Web Services has agreed to provide U.S. government agencies with up to $1 billion in savings."
Amazon "agreed" to help the U.S. government, not the other way around. Starlink effectively
decides whether or not the internet remains functional for either side in Ukraine. SpaceX is,
of course, far more powerful than its governmental counterpart, NASA.

My analysis is that such a reorganization is not optional. As we transition from a print-based
culture to a digital one, many institutions tailored for the former are becoming obsolete. Today's
world order emerged from the Thirty Years' War, which was itself a consequence of a revolution in
communication: the printing press led to Protestantism, which in turn sparked the European wars of
religion.

If the ground under our political institutions is shifting, then the individual experience of our
shared world must shift too. The crumbling is not just institutional — it is also going to be
phenomenological.

## Lost

I expect "weird times" ahead, where one part of the population clings to the ancient model while
another has already migrated to a digital culture. This switch is occurring across multiple scales
and domains simultaneously.

I still see people wearing masks today. To me, they are living in a completely separate reality.
This kind of perceptual gap is about to manifest across the entire world, on countless subjects,
all at once.

The fall of the Roman Empire was more like the collapse of a bridge that had long suffered from
a lack of maintenance. No one was awakened in the middle of the night by a "Breaking News: Roman
Empire Has Fallen" tweet. However, the situation today is not quite so simple: digital technologies
act as multipliers and accelerators. This time, it may be different.

Serious thinkers will find themselves lost in a labyrinth of competing propositions. Which ones
make sense? Which will survive the next news cycle? What are the core beliefs of my interlocutor?
What kind of world are our descendants going to inherit?

A Maze Inc., isn't it?
