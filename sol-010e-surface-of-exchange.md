  <key_concepts>
    <concept name="Exchange Surface — Quantitative Explosion / Qualitative Collapse">
      The central metaphor: social media dramatically widened the "exchange surface" —
      the area of possible interaction between individuals. Direct interaction with people
      previously watchable only on TV became everyday experience. But the explosion in
      quantity was accompanied by a transformation in quality: most interactions are now
      mediated not by another human but by an algorithm (the feed-building AI). The
      exchange surface is vast but the actual exchange partner is increasingly non-human.
      "More than social dynamic, the interactions with artificial intelligence has to be
      analyzed and understood." This is the conceptual seed of sol-042 (Dark Dorian:
      the animated algorithmic shadow-self) and sol-024h (TR Goes Mental: the AI as
      the actual interlocutor in semantic space).
    </concept>
    <concept name="The Eternal Present — Social Media as Pascal Inverted">
      The article cites Pascal's Pensées: humans never live in the present — only in
      the past (memory) or future (hope). Social media inverts this: it creates an
      eternal present. The raw stream of information (Ukraine war footage arriving
      unedited, live) eliminates editorialization and therefore context — past and future
      vanish, leaving only the immediate. "Over focus on the feelings captures your
      thoughts and mechanically erases the past and the future." This is the mechanism
      behind sol-039 (Hyperpresent) and sol-043 (Babel Timeline: WWIII-scale events
      share timeline space with restaurant reviews). The Pascal inversion is elegantly
      precise: the pre-digital human struggled to live in the present; the digital human
      is trapped in it.
    </concept>
    <concept name="Babel Tower Extended — Group Dynamics of Fame">
      Using Haidt's Babel Tower allegory, the article adds a dimension Haidt did not
      develop: the tower becomes so tall that people at the top lose common language
      with those at the bottom. Masons at the top order bricks, receive mortar.
      Applied to social media: the virtual world is so decoupled from physical reality
      that its inhabitants lose the ability to communicate with those still grounded
      in material constraints. "Like the ones on the top of the Babel Tower, we are
      having more and more trouble to have a common language." This Babel dynamic
      prefigures sol-043 (The Babel Timeline) — the title and central metaphor of that
      article finds its genealogy here. Also: fame dynamics on social media produce
      "narcissism of small differences" (Haidt's term adopted by the author) that
      shatters shared narrative into competing niche bubbles.
    </concept>

<content>
Written as a response to Jonathan Haidt's "Why the Past 10 Years of American Life Have Been
Uniquely Stupid" (The Atlantic, April 2022), this article adds three dimensions to Haidt's
Babel Tower analysis:

EXCHANGE SURFACE: social media exploded the surface of possible human exchange (direct
interaction with TV celebrities became everyday experience) while simultaneously degrading
its quality. The reason: most interactions are now mediated not by another human but by
an algorithm. "More than social dynamic, the interactions with artificial intelligence has
to be analyzed and understood." The real interlocutor on social media is increasingly the
feed-building AI — the conceptual seed of Dark Dorian (sol-042) and the Second Topological
Revolution (sol-024h).

PASCAL INVERTED — THE ETERNAL PRESENT: Pascal observed that humans live in the past
(memory) or future (hope) but never in the present. Social media inverts this: raw,
unedited information streams (Ukraine war footage arriving live) eliminate editorialization
and context, trapping the viewer in an eternal, emotion-saturated present. "Over focus on
the feelings captures your thoughts and mechanically erases the past and the future."
The mechanism behind the Hyperpresent (sol-039) and the Babel Timeline (sol-043).

BABEL EXTENDED: the tower becomes so tall that those at the top lose common language
with those at the bottom. Masons order bricks, receive mortar. Virtual decoupling from
physical reality produces the same failure of common language — the Babel dynamic that
sol-043 names directly. Fame dynamics produce "narcissism of small differences" that
shatters shared narrative into competing niche bubbles.

Performing vs connecting: Haidt's observation that platforms "trained users to spend more
time performing and less time connecting" is confirmed and extended. Performance (visibility
seeking) is structurally incentivized; connection (genuine exchange) is structurally punished
by the same algorithm that rewards performance metrics.
