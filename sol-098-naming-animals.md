# Naming Animals

A new round of riots is unfolding in France (along with Italy, Albania, and somehow Belgium too). These riots, at least in France, are not like the usual ones. The phrase "fiery but mostly peaceful protests" has become a meme in France, but this time is different. As a French person, I have lived through several waves of unrest. Even though the writing was already on the wall after the previous major chapters in 2018 and 2023, this one might be the real deal. The images of the blazing Limoges town hall and the Lyon tourist office (where I own an apartment just about 100 meters away) have made international front pages, but I think the situation is best illustrated by this short video of two lost, poor Asian tourists

[Embedded X post]

The images speak for themselves. The fact that they are two dots of color lost in a sea of rioters dressed in black is visually striking and serves as the perfect allegory: we are in an era where parallel worlds cross paths without a shred of mutual understanding. This is the visual counterpart to the conclusion of *A Maze Inc :*

> This is why I believe the current zeitgeist will be defined by a profound sense of being lost. The sheer multiplicity of visions emanating from these major players will not help. As I have noted, expect hundreds of similar statements to emerge in short order. These manifestos will convince some while alienating others, creating yet another chasm in the public sphere—much like how your social media feed is no longer the same as mine.
>
> If this leads merely to misunderstanding—and frankly, misunderstanding might be the best-case scenario—for many others, it will lead to the feeling of being trapped in a maze of powerful actors who clearly do not share the same worldview. It will be like a conversation between a Taliban fighter and an LGBTQ+ activist. Everywhere. All the time.

Because, apart from the "usual" pictures of riots, what emerges from all of this is… nothing. Let me be clear: as I said, I have witnessed a lot of protests. It is almost a rite of passage for everyone in France. Each time, there was a core idea that emerged, and you could sum it up in a slogan or a single issue. That is not the case here. Everyone comes with their own demands, and it all merges into one giant, chaotic mess. It is a perfect expression of what I call the hyperpresent.

We are in a hyper-connected era: every riot is recorded and streamed live, and yet there is not a single narrative. The death of mainstream media is, of course, not helping, but it goes beyond the mere inability to manufacture consent in a digital culture (which in itself is a huge deal). Life has become this giant social media mess that, unlike the virtual version, is not sorted by an algorithm. The top-down narrative infrastructure is dead, but there is no proper distributed infrastructure yet for a global narrative. Everyone comes with their own explanation of the why and the what to do: Palestine, the cost of living, institutional apathy, the emptiness of the political class, and fear of the future—whether driven by AI or climate change… You can even spot them by their look—at least for a French person, it takes about 0.3 seconds to decode. Yet, they are all converging into a giant, never-ending protest, which is another defining feature of this round of riots. Unlike usual protests, the looting continued sporadically on Sunday and Monday, even though the main event was on Saturday. Groups of looters gather here and there and get to work.

Another unusual aspect is how the crisis is being handled by the powers that be. On Sunday, October 18th, Macron gave an 8-minute-and-35-second speech in which he did nothing more than prove he couldn't care less. It was the bare minimum. Almost everything he said could have been said during any other round of riots, except for the mention of the upcoming presidential elections (for which he cannot run again after two consecutive terms). Even the commander-in-chief can't find the words to describe what he is facing, and what he has become. "Elusive" is the word that comes to mind.

> Now the LORD God had formed out of the ground all the wild animals and all the birds in the sky. He brought them to the man to see what he would name them; and whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name. So the man gave names to all the livestock, the birds in the sky and all the wild animals. But for Adam no suitable helper was found
>
> Genesis 2:19-20

We are at a stage where “no suitable helper” could be found. In the Bible, the continuation of the story is the creation of woman and community: the family as the cornerstone of living together. But first, man falls into a deep sleep. Maybe that is where we are now? Are we ready for the removal of a rib? That sounds plausible: there is a price to pay to enter the new digital era, and that price cannot be borrowed from the markets.

In another mythological tradition of the West—the *Iliad* and the *Odyssey*—renewal comes through a decade of fighting and an equally long period of wandering at sea. We only had one episode of looting last Saturday. It is just the beginning of the beginning. There will be enough adventures to fill volumes to come before we reach a new normal. On a side note, I am already wondering where to park my car in the street, thinking about next Saturday.
