# The Social Kaleidoscope

*‘Are you ready?’* translates to ‘*¿Estás listo?’*in Spanish. The etymology is clear: despite their apparent similarity, there is no link between the English word ‘list’ and the Spanish word ‘listo’.

[Embedded X post]

Yet you have to be ready, because we are entering a new phase of political life. The mere fact that the Democrats are mentioning a target list for after the elections couldn’t be more blunt. Western *politeia* is dead. The X post above reads like a death certificate, but the writing has been on the wall for some time now, for those who can read at least.

### Historians and narratives

Historians build narratives based on sources. Most of them focus on events like wars, revolutions, and major social movements. The astute reader will have noted that the entire structure rests on the foundation of ‘sources.’ So perhaps a better angle for considering the historical story of humanity is to focus on the pivotal role of information technologies—after all, what are sources if not products of information technology? The very tool through which History, with a capital ‘H’, is told appears to be a very good candidate for the secret engine of History itself.

If I am mentioning this, it is because the historical arc unfolding since the fall of communism seems to be powered by the revolution in information technologies at least as much as other forces, such as the transformation of the labor market or the revolution in travel. Note that the fall of the Berlin Wall occurred at the very moment cable news networks like CNN took over. Remember how CNN brought global attention to the student-led protests in Beijing in 1989. Even when Chinese authorities ordered CNN to cease broadcasting, reporter Bernard Shaw famously continued reporting live over the telephone, establishing CNN’s reputation for on-the-ground international journalism. In a sense—and given that the Chinese regime, unlike the USSR, did not fall—this shows that the evolution of information technology is a more powerful framework than traditional geopolitical events for explaining large-scale transformations.

I am not going to write a book about the rise of the internet, the dot-com bubble, and the social media era: this Substack is here for that. What I want to stress here is that we have reached what appears to be a tipping point between a written(print)-based culture and a digital-based culture. Our very understanding of what politics is is shifting. Forget the left/right divide. The question is no longer about the economic structure of societies—even if some are still stuck in 19th-century theories—but about the very structure of institutions. The “target list” is an explicit illustration of this idea: the democratic process is explicitly viewed as a way to enforce the views of your faction. The public sphere is no longer perceived as a shared, neutral playing field. Winning elections merely becomes the right to impose views by force. ‘Tyranny of the majority’ comes to mind, but it is worse. *Tranny* of the majority sounds more apt in this woke 2.0 era.

Just like the Terms of Service (TOS) of your preferred social media platform can be changed overnight, a change of administration brings the total destruction of whatever the previous one accomplished. It is like Penelope’s unraveling shroud—you undo what was just done. Progress is stuck. In fact, it is worse: the prevailing sentiment is one of vengeance. It is no longer enough to undo what has been done; revenge is on the horizon. The “list of targets” is the menu.

### The means of production

In this information-centered alternative to historical materialism, the means of production are not those of workers or factories—we are talking about production in the cinematic sense. It is therefore no surprise that looters loot with smartphones in hand, broadcasting live on Twitch. Likewise, mainstream media is incapable of driving the narrative; algorithms and social media platforms are leading the way.

This is likely a major reason why the ongoing social unrest in France—which is spreading rapidly to other countries like Italy, Albania, and Belgium—remains so elusive to define. Indeed, many factions that have almost nothing in common are taking part. Yet, each faction views the unrest solely through the lens of its own social media feed. Each faction owns its means of media production:

1. **Looters:** They experience the unrest as a giant, never-ending razzia/looting spree. Imagine the aftermath of a PSG Champions League victory turned into a permanent lifestyle. There is even a Wikipedia entry for it: *2026 Paris Saint-Germain celebration riots*.
2. **Students:** For the nth time, they are re-enacting “May ‘68.” It is disappointing to witness Gen-Z’s lack of creativity; Boomers are truly living rent-free in their minds. Nothing substantial—or going beyond “we want more free stuff”—emerges from their demonstrations.
3. **The Far-Left:** By hook or by crook, the far-left has never been particularly subtle about its “the ends justify the means” approach to seizing political power. Naturally, they don’t want to be bothered by technical details, preferring to leave the practical aspects to a bureaucracy. Power is their sole objective. Jean-Luc Mélenchon’s statement is clear: *“Il va falloir qu’ils obéissent”* (”They’re going to have to obey”)—a remarkably concise executive summary of his platform.
4. **The Gilets Jaunes**: They are the true 2026 lumpenproletariat. A reboot of the 2018–2019 movement is underway. Next Saturday has already been named ‘Acte II,’ just like during the first season when they used to number every call to demonstrate. They are ordinary, working-class people strangled by living conditions. Having gained nothing from the 2018 edition, they are back with a vengeance. Their focus is on gas prices and the cost of living. Politically speaking, there is nothing deeper here—just angry people. Much like the students: ‘we want more free stuff.’

The natural question is: what can be expected from a revolutionary movement based on such a fragmented absence of ideology?

### The Social Kaleidoscope

Early Marxist theory and its later iterations (Lenin, Trotsky, Mao) included the idea of a global revolution. These evolutions were Procrustean adaptations of the initial theory, which had to be stretched because reality never ceased to invalidate the theory. In our parallel, information-driven history it translates into the Omnicrisis: the global conflagration to come. Things evolve in real time, and I hope that it will be funny to re-read this text few months/years from now: but the news of the day is that you have to be ready to defend yourself. It is an official statement by the Préfet de Police de Paris (Paris Police commisioner):

[Embedded X post]

Translation : *The Paris Police Prefecture is advising local business owners to hire private security guards ahead of the upcoming demonstrations***.**

I also must admit that I never imagined that such a day would come. If the french state is obese, it has always be very efficient in two domains: organized force and tax recovery. So we are truly entering uncharted territory. The fact that we do it in an advanced state of disunity is not a good omen. The truly new thing is that the efficiency of new technologies may allow us to remain in a state of disunion for longer than it had ever been possible. That explains why I cannot imagine what is going to happen in the coming weeks. The only thing I know is that several parallel segments of the population will live in different realities. Is it going to converge on a winner-take-all situation? Is it going to continue for weeks, months, and through the next administration (there are presidential elections in less than six months)? Who knows?

**Every man for himself, and God for us all**
