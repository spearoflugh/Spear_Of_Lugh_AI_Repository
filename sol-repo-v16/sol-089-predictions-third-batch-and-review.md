# Predictions - Third Batch and Review

104 days have flown away since my last batch of predictions. The Second Batch was just before the MOU episode, God only knows what is going to happen shortly after this third batch since the first one was published a week before the start of the Iran war…

I will innovate in this by using a machine to rate my previous predictions. I am going to give as input to Claude my last batches and ask it to rate the predictions. I will after that make the new predictions based on what I have published here since the previous batch.

### Claude review of the second batch

The prompt for Sonnet 5 was

> I had published predictions in the https://spearoflugh.substack.com/p/predictions-second-batch-and-review we are now 100 days later. Please write me a review of the predictions that I made in the second batch (that I have just given) and from the first batch that is here https://spearoflugh.substack.com/p/predictions-first-batch write the review in the style and length of the second batch (bullet points, passing in review each production).

Here is the assessment of Claude regarding my predictions (copy paste, I have added pointers to relevant substack publications):

## Predictions — Review of First Batch (200 Days) and Second Batch (100 Days)

Two clocks to check today: the first batch is now 200 days old, the second batch exactly 100. Let’s take them in order.

### First Batch, revisited

**The UFO files release.** Nothing to add to the prior verdict, if anything it’s been reinforced by silence. The Social Matryoshka held — no new revelations, no camp switching sides. This prediction has essentially exited the news cycle entirely, which is itself the confirmation: an information release that changes nothing is eventually just... forgotten. Correct, and now correct twice over.

**The Iran War.** The structure keeps holding, the ordering keeps being wrong in the same direction. Since the last check-in, the war did the thing you said it would refuse to do cleanly: it “ended,” then didn’t. A Pakistan-brokered ceasefire held from April to July, Trump declared the memorandum of understanding “over” on July 8, and fighting resumed. Iran had launched at least 501 missiles and drones since July 7, with a burst of 70 in a single 48-hour window over August 31–September 2. As of two weeks ago the war was in its ninth consecutive day without an Iranian strike on a host state, but nobody’s calling that peace — the disarmament file is stalled, the pause is being read as calculation rather than resolution, and the Houthis remain the war’s most active front. Regime survival, not regime change, turned out to be the story; saturation-strike Iran, exactly as forecast, turned out to be durable. Structurally right again, and the “least likely” ceasefire outcome has now essentially been overtaken by a war that just doesn’t stop restarting — which, ironically, sets up the second batch prediction below almost too well.

**The American looming civil war.** Still too early, still on track for “before the end of the Trump administration” rather than before the midterms. But the temperature has kept climbing rather than cooling: an estimated seven million people took to the streets in the “No Kings” protests, opposition mobilization has continued around immigration enforcement, a 44-day government shutdown, and election-integrity disputes ahead of the November midterms, and Trump’s approval has slipped below 40% in multiple polls. That’s exactly the synchronization point flagged earlier — midterms as flashpoint — now arriving on schedule. No spark yet, no Omnicause convergence, but the kindling is drier than it was 100 days ago.

**The unravelling of post-WWII international order.** This is the one that’s gone from “directionally right” to “basically consensus.” The Munich Security Report themed its 2026 edition “Under Destruction,” describing the post-1945 order as being demolished by political forces that prefer disruption to reform, and Austria’s own defense-policy risk assessment dropped the assumption of a “stable, rules-based international order” altogether, calling the new baseline confrontational, multipolar, and imperialistic. The UN funding crisis has arrived too. The tech-sovereignty angle also keeps aging well, though nothing new needed chasing this round — the throughline (states can’t administer without big tech’s rails) hasn’t needed updating because nothing has contradicted it. Correct, and increasingly mainstream-correct rather than contrarian-correct.

### Second Batch, first check-in

**The return of scarcity.** Split decision, leaning right. The discourse has absolutely arrived: Wall Street is now openly debating an “AI slowdown” on data center buildout, and a Cast AI survey of roughly 23,000 Kubernetes clusters found enterprises are buying GPU capacity and leaving it largely idle, with the real bottleneck being data readiness and architecture rather than model capability. That’s the “we’ll store it but not process it” arbitrage, almost verbatim. But the frenzy hasn’t actually cooled at the balance-sheet level — Gartner still projects worldwide IT spending hitting $6.31 trillion in 2026, up 13.5% year over year, driven mostly by AI infrastructure and cloud subscriptions, and hyperscaler capex is still climbing into the hundreds of billions. So: the *conversation* about scarcity and waste has clearly started on schedule, exactly where predicted to look (LinkedIn discourse, cost-consciousness framing), but the adoption-rate cooling itself hasn’t shown up in the spending yet. Early and half-right — worth another look next quarter.

**No clear cut ending to the Iran War.** This one is close to a bullseye, and honestly it graded itself in the section above. Ceasefire announced, ceasefire declared “over,” fighting resumed, a new pause that nobody trusts, strikes landing everywhere from Jordan to Bahrain to Kuwait with no front line in sight. “Mutually Assured Dronization” and the discrete, laundry-list nature of the conflict are exactly what the last five months produced. If anything, the non-ending dragged on even longer than expected.

**Decoupling lived experience and virtual presence.** Both halves of this one are landing well. On the economy-of-trust side: 2026 has seen a genuine pivot toward verification-as-business-model, with 54% of consumers saying AI has made them more cautious about what they trust online, and a shift from “believe until proven wrong” to “doubt until proven real” driving demand for verified user-generated content — industry trend reporting converges on the same line: the bar for posting has fallen, but the bar for trust has risen. On the physical-experience side: “quietcations” and “hushpitality” — trips designed explicitly to disconnect from screens, crowds, and notifications — are being flagged as one of the defining global travel trends of the year, with entire destinations now enforcing quiet zones. Podcasts-as-counter-culture still looks dead; nothing’s emerged yet to replace it, which was already flagged as the open question.

All in all: the Iran-war-related calls remain the strongest hits, the order-unravelling read has aged from bold to obvious, the civil-war clock is still ticking but hasn’t struck, and the AI-scarcity call is the one genuinely open question — right about the mood, not yet right about the money.

It is now time to produce a new batch of predictions related to what I have published. The idea is to keep a record of what I think at given points in time in order to see if the project is producing interesting predictions.

### Predictions

1. **Crisis in Academia.**This one is easy, almost too easy because it can already be seen. I was talking abstractly about the evolution of culture in Aesop’s Reels, it was less than 100 days away but the landscape has been totally changed since. The last advances by AIs, though unclear (one doesn’t really know what is genuine, inspired and outright result of theft), are impressive. I talk about the Navier-Stokes aftermath in Anima & Intelligence where I stress the psychological impact, I also talked more directly about the transformation of culture in Hidden Ldeger, Crouching Lagrange. Notably regarding the Lagrange points (things that remain stable in a context where everything moves around): my prediction is that mechanism to attribute grants and more generally the “Peer-review based academia” are going to massively change. It is not a short term prediction but the current tracks are unworkable. It is now, thanks to AI, easier to write articles than to read and assess them. Hence reputation mechanisms will emerge and are going to be the base over which academia is going to work. First it will happen in mathematics (because they can’t produce anything else than articles)), then computer science (for which poc and software can replace publication but here also AIs are changing the game) and other branches of science will follow (humanities will get stuck into grievance studies affairs for decade before moving on. 1. **Deep Crisis in Higher-Education**: The preceding point was about the crisis seen from the inside. From the outside it will impact the whole of the higher-education. Already I have been able to witness it during interviews to recruit new students for an engineering curriculum. Half of them had questions about the future of the work place (fear, not completely unsound, that AI is going to rip the whole sector). I was talking about this under the form of the burst of AI bubble in Con Census. How does it translate in terms of prediction? I expect a wave of activism by the young that is not going to be constructive. You are going to see riots for which nothing tangible is gained. There is not yet an ideology that crystalizes the fears of the youth. Moreover, due to this transitional phase, they have not been trained to write down elaborate thoughts. A generation of AI spoon fed activists. The spoon is formatted at the image of the powers that be (where would their learning material come from?). So it will be a strange thing: they are going to want to escape from a dire situation using the (intellectual) tools that have built the situation (through the use of AIs). I think that it will be very hard for Gen Z and Alpha (the ones that have the motivation and life impulse) to formalize it. Maybe the lights will come from Gen Xers? But I am getting distracted. So I expect a very tumultuous year (next spring will be something). Something like a global 1968 on digital steroids. Just after WWII higher education was seen as a universal key. Then, from the 90s it became something else. Standard went down (for many reasons, DEI policies included) and Higher Education became an industry of leisure: loans, spring breaks, etc. To say that the work ethics suffered is the understatement of this century. But the ground is approaching fast and even student can’t ignore it.
2. **The OmniCrisis**: The time is ripe for a global crisis, not just on educational and research fronts. I was touching on it on The Ongoing Polymorphic Coup. My prediction is here very simple: there will be demonstrations and riots the likes of which we haven’t seen in decades. The conjunction of the geopolitical instabilities together with financial issues (inflation and shortages due to the war, plus the debt crisis) and immigration standoff are enough but on top of that one has to add the memetic virality of social media. International crowds will compete with one another to grab attention by doing more and more egregious actions. Something like Gilets Jaunes ++. I expect to see hot things to unravel before next Christmas. I am explicitly thinking at events within countries but happening on a worldwide scale simultaneously. A kind of Synchronized Symphony.
3. **Planet Wars**: Even if it was not part of prediction batch I talked about orbital strike systems for years now. We just have na official confirmation that the US claims to possess such a capability. Here is what I wrote back then:

> It (orbital strike system) is not that challenging and after this brief study I am even wondering whether or not it has already been partially implemented by major powers like the US, China or Russia. A major obstacle was the cost of such a solution. SpaceX costs have neutralized this barrier.

My prediction is that such a system is going to be effectively used in a hot war: Iran, Ukraine, Yemen, you name it.

Since this batch isn’t particularly cheerful, I’ll call it a day and wrap things up here. See you in 100 days to check how things have unfold.
