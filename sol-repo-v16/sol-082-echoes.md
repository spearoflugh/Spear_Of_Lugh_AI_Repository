# Echoes

In Greek mythology, Echo is cursed for having talked too much, thereby distracting Hera and allowing Zeus to have earthly adventures. Hera's curse was to deprive Echo of an original voice; she was only able to repeat the last words she heard. Any resemblance to social media is not coincidental.

On social media, users are cursed by the design of the system. Visibility is given only to those talking about the latest trend. Ironically, it is this mechanism that produces distraction on a massive scale. Another case of reversal due to digital technologies: the cause of the curse has become its outcome.

In mythology, the curse forbids Echo from declaring her love for Narcissus. She dies of sorrow. The question is: what is this curse doing at a societal level in a digital culture?

### Lack of intent

A sure sign of a bad chess player is the lack of intent in their play. At each step, the decisions taken appear reasonable, but taking a step back reveals a much grimmer picture: moves cancel one another and nothing is achieved. When faced with a competent enemy, defeat is certain. In the case of chess, it stems from a lack of the big picture. Decisions are made locally, without a global plan. On social media, it is a never-ending stream of daily topics, even as we are in the midst of a civilizational shift the likes of which we haven’t experienced for millennia (the shift from oral to written cultures).

The lack of will in her communication is the worst part of Echo's curse. She has lost control of her words. Likewise, on a social media platform, one has to surrender the choice of the subject in order to be heard. The only way to avoid the echo is to speak into the void. Nothing will reverberate, but the question of whether a tree has fallen if no one was there to hear it remains. Was the word said if it was never listened to?

The semi-happy news is that at least robots will hear you talking and absorb your words as learning material. When everything that has been published has already been scraped, they will wander around your social media accounts like vampires in search of true blood. But is being a data point hidden inside a huge matrix of weights the same as being heard? Aren't you going to die of sorrow as in the myth?

### Beginning and end

“In the beginning was the Word” is the opener of John 1:1. Echo inverts that. Not a good omen. The Word—or the last words—is the ultimate thing that remains. Its importance decays over time. Think of all the earth-shattering stories of the last decade: Epstein, UFOs, Biden’s decline during his last term, the COVID crisis, mass immigration, etc. These stories come and go as trends on social media when new files are released or hearings are held. The repetition itself makes them less noteworthy to users.

​Another impact of repetition is that it takes time. Just like Narcissus spending all his time looking at his reflection, repeating words consumes energy, and the only things left are words. This explains the growing disconnect felt between real life and the internet. It is more than a feeling. Dopamine crash as a way of life.

### GLP-1

There is no known cure for the curse of Echo. Because she is a mountain nymph, she is an immortal divine spirit rather than a mortal human. She never really dies. Eternity lasts long especially at the end.

What does it means in terms of social media? The first idea is that the echo malediction is consubstantial to this form of communication. There is no silver bullet. More than that, every magic pill (for instance limiting the age of users to be over 15 as it is going to happen in France from September 1st.) isn’t going to work. Most probably they will come with horrendous side effects that no one saw coming.

So is there a way out? The aim of communication is to allow people to cooperate. Bitcoin was an interesting try at decentralized cooperation, but it was limited to the transfer of money—an important case, for sure, but a limited one. The marketplace of ideas doesn’t work like one for commodities. It is not a zero-sum game. When you send one Bitcoin, there is one less Bitcoin in your account. It doesn’t work like that for conversations and ideas. That applies to the positive, but also to the negative: bad-faith interlocutors can derail a conversation as surely as night follows day. Having a reputation system that makes it possible to spot bad-faith actors would be a good step toward helping platforms handle this. But it is neither a simple task nor a magic pill; humans are masters at gaming reputation systems. This heralds another eternal arms race between the sword and the shield.
