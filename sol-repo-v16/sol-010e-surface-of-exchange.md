  <key_concepts>
    <concept name="Exchange Surface — Quantitative Explosion / Qualitative Collapse">
      The central metaphor: social media dramatically widened the "exchange surface" —
      the area of possible interaction between individuals. Direct interaction with people
      previously watchable only on TV became everyday experience. But the explosion in
      quantity was accompanied by a transformation in quality: most interactions are now
      mediated not by another human but by an algorithm (the feed-building AI). The
      exchange surface is vast but the actual exchange partner is increasingly non-human.
      "More than social dynamic, the interactions with artificial intelligence has to be
      analyzed and understood." This is the conceptual seed of sol-042 (Dark Dorian:
      the animated algorithmic shadow-self) and sol-024h (TR Goes Mental: the AI as
      the actual interlocutor in semantic space).
    </concept>
    <concept name="The Eternal Present — Social Media as Pascal Inverted">
      The article cites Pascal's Pensées: humans never live in the present — only in
      the past (memory) or future (hope). Social media inverts this: it creates an
      eternal present. The raw stream of information (Ukraine war footage arriving
      unedited, live) eliminates editorialization and therefore context — past and future
      vanish, leaving only the immediate. "Over focus on the feelings captures your
      thoughts and mechanically erases the past and the future." This is the mechanism
      behind sol-039 (Hyperpresent) and sol-043 (Babel Timeline: WWIII-scale events
      share timeline space with restaurant reviews). The Pascal inversion is elegantly
      precise: the pre-digital human struggled to live in the present; the digital human
      is trapped in it.
    </concept>
    <concept name="Babel Tower Extended — Group Dynamics of Fame">
      Using Haidt's Babel Tower allegory, the article adds a dimension Haidt did not
      develop: the tower becomes so tall that people at the top lose common language
      with those at the bottom. Masons at the top order bricks, receive mortar.
      Applied to social media: the virtual world is so decoupled from physical reality
      that its inhabitants lose the ability to communicate with those still grounded
      in material constraints. "Like the ones on the top of the Babel Tower, we are
      having more and more trouble to have a common language." This Babel dynamic
      prefigures sol-043 (The Babel Timeline) — the title and central metaphor of that
      article finds its genealogy here. Also: fame dynamics on social media produce
      "narcissism of small differences" (Haidt's term adopted by the author) that
      shatters shared narrative into competing niche bubbles.
    </concept>

# The Surface of Exchange

Jonathan Haidt Article

In "Why the Past 10 Years of American Life Have Been Uniquely Stupid", Jonathan Haidt tackles with the societal effects of social media. This is the exact angle of this substack. It is an issue on which I have spent a lot of thinking during those last years. I address here some comments and remarks that this read inspired me, as well as some new ideas that it generated.

### The Babel allegory

J. Haidt uses the allegory of the Babel Tower to describe the situation. It is a very compelling case: the dream of a Kumbaya moment, fueled by the hopes of a renewal of democracy brought by new information technologies, has been gradually replaced with a fractured society. Mis/dis/malinformation rule the day. This is the first idea developped by J. Haidt: it is not because you are using information technologies that you are building bridges between individual. The following quote is crystal clear:

> Once social-media platforms had trained users to spend more time performing and less time connecting

This is an aspect of social media that I had already spotted. I used the myth of Dorian Gray , one can also think at the Snow White mirror. The Babel myth adds this idea of group dynamics that lacks to both the Dorian Gray and Snow White stories. Emotions are not limited to individuals. As a participant of social media you also broadcast approval and disapproval points. The reciprocity law implies that if you want to be liked, you have to like. It creates the conditions of a never ending race for internet celebrity that favours passions over reflections.

This is the part of the story where the Babel Tower falls down. Everyone can become famous, but in order to become famous there has to be something peculiar to pinpoint to. "Narcissism of small differences" may come to mind. This is this mechanism that shatters the common story into a multitude of niche celebrity bubles and competing tribes.

There is maybe another idea in the Babel story that may help to understand our times that J. Haidt has not developped. The Tower becomes so big that people at the top are no longer linked to the people at the bottom. There are versions of the stories where the masons at the top oder bricks and recieve mortar instead. It illustrates well how much our virtual lives are cut off from reality. I am sure there is something there that explains a part of the madness we are experiencing. In a virtual world the only limit is the one of your imagination. You can choose your gender and things like that on a whim. Having too much of our lives spent in a virtual environment is playing with our perception of the world. Like the ones on the top of the Babel Tower, we are having more and more trouble to have a common language with the ones at the bottom of it.

### On the internet no one remember you have ever screamed

I do agree that group dynamics (virtue signaling and tribe behaviors) is important, but I think that the most important feature of the digital cutlure is its lack of stability. The social media time is the present. Not the past, not the future but an eternal present. This is strange. Consider this following quote:

> “Que chacun examine ses pensées. Il les trouvera toutes occupées au passé ou à l'avenir. Nous ne pensons presque point au présent, et si nous y pensons ce n'est que pour en prendre la lumière pour disposer de l'avenir, le présent n'est jamais notre fin.
>
> Le passé et le présent sont nos moyens ; le seul avenir est notre fin. Ainsi nous ne vivons jamais, mais nous espérons de vivre”
>
> (free traduction) Let everyone examine their thoughts. He will find them all occupied with the past or the future. We hardly think of the present, and if we think of it it is only to take light of it to dispose of the future, the present is never our end. The past and the present are our means; the only future is our end. So we never live, but we hope to live
>
> Pascal - Pensées

Once again we are facing a phenomenon that is an upside-down version of what it used to be before the avent of the digital culture. Now that the spread of information is instantaneous we are stuck in a never ending stream of information coming from any place in the globe. Think about the war in Ukraine. Social media made the conflict to appear in your home live, without filter. It just produced a raw stream of images directly available for comsumption. There was no editorialization of the information like it was the case with TV broadcasts or newspapers. You have the feeling that you are experiencing the thing not that you are reading a report about the thing. This over focus on the feelings capture your thoughts and mechanically erase the past and the future. It becomes harder to step back from the spectacle and put it into context. ### The exchange surface

A last idea that this reading produced is this idea of exchange surface. Social media has offered a huge widening of the exchange capabilities between individuals. Direct interactions with people you could only watch on TV is an everyday experience. Notice how *celebrity* has evolved during those last two decades. There are less and less A style celebrities (famous actors etc.). Keeping up with the Kardashians is an epitome of this transition to the niche celebrities of the XXI°. But this explosion in quantity has been accompanied by a tranformation in quality. Most discussions, and a lot of people you meet online, are mediated via an artificial intelligence. This is the software that builds your feed which has become your window on the world. So more than social dynamic, the interactions with artificial intelligence has to be analyzed and understood. This is quite new and hard to grasp because, by definition artificial engines never stay the same they evolve as they learn (they are designed to do that!).

We find ourselves in a really new environment where many of our intuitions on how things work can be proven wrong in the snap of a finger. I think there are positive aspects of this new culture. Spotting the dangers and imagining how we could mitigate their influence should be our priority.
