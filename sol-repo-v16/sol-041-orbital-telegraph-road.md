# Orbital Telegraph Road

SpaceX and xAI have just announced their merger into a single entity. True to form, the move comes with an “Elonesque” plan to deploy millions of satellites dedicated to AI processing. Naturally, significant hurdles remain—most notably the self-evident challenges of thermal management in a vacuum and orbital congestion—and Musk is famous for his hyperbolic business promises.

Yet, Musk is also known for delivering earth-shattering technologies. Starlink is a reality, already operating thousands of satellites. Furthermore, SpaceX is currently launching more mass into orbit than any other player in human history. To put it in perspective:

> In 2025 alone, SpaceX delivered approximately 2,413 metric tons of payload to orbit—exceeding China’s cumulative total of roughly 1,812 metric tons across its entire space program history (from 1970 through the end of 2025).

Read that last sentence again, slowly, to let the scale sink in. Those are no longer just hyperbolic promises but facts.

We are witnessing a shift in the space industry that will fundamentally alter life on Earth. These changes aren’t incremental; they are a “French Revolution 4.0” on AI steroids. While I have previously written on the implications of space exploration through the lens of information technology and strategic/military perspectives [here] and [here], this piece will focus on the expected societal impacts for our digital civilization.

### Topological Equality

The first step of the digital revolution was transforming speech—which once possessed the qualities of an **object**—into something that possesses all the qualities of an **idea**. Since Plato, we have known that objects and ideas do not share the same properties. For instance, an object can be physically located; an idea is everywhere and nowhere at once. If an object is stolen, its absence is immediately visible. If a secret is stolen, how can you even tell? I could go on for pages on these distinctions, but the point is clear: new information technologies act as a portal between the “ideal” world and the “objective” world (I have written more on that [here]).

One concrete implication of this shift is the loss of locality of speech. Before the digital age, information moved at the speed of objects; orders and armies marched at the same pace. The digital revolution shattered that harmony. Suddenly, speech acquired the properties of an idea. To put it bluntly: a tweet can be read instantaneously by anyone, anywhere. Yet, despite our progress in transport, humans must still move using relatively slow machines. A fundamental disharmony was introduced: words and swords no longer move at the same pace.

The space industry is now promising to resolve this disharmony. It is a banal but profound observation: every point on Earth is at an equal distance from orbit. For a satellite in polar orbit, every inch of the planet eventually passes beneath it. This may sound abstract, but the implications are incredibly concrete. GPS and Starlink are truly global services precisely because they leverage this geometric property.

Space technology is a revolution that promises to make every point on Earth **topologically equal**. It is as radical as the French Revolution, which sought to make every citizen legally equal by abolishing the monarchy. To be more precise: **the space revolution is completing the digital revolution, but on the objective side of reality.**

Again if you think  these are merely theoretical ramblings, look at how the Russian Army is utilizing with Starlink on the battlefield. This isn’t science fiction;It is hic et nunc. And yet it is just the beginning.

### Builders and Rulers

News stories have a way of shoving a dreamer’s face back into the biting cold of reality. The merger between SpaceX and xAI occurred at the exact moment French police were searching X’s Paris headquarters last Tuesday (February 3rd, 2026), as prosecutors issued a summons for the company’s owner, Elon Musk.

There is another striking reality to consider: In 2025, the European Union significantly escalated its financial pressure on Big Tech. Under the massive enforcement of the Digital Markets Act (DMA) and the Digital Services Act (DSA), the EU issued more than—are you sitting down?—€4.5 billion in fines.

Now, make sure you’re *really* sitting down. The total revenue from Digital Services Taxes across Europe amounted to €3.5 billion. Yes, you read that correctly: the EU currently generates more revenue from fining extra-European companies (since there are no world-scale internet giants based in Europe—perhaps Spotify?) than it does from taxing them.

If you want a sense of the disparity, consider this: last year, all of Europe combined put 2.1 metric tons into orbit. That is a thousand times less than SpaceX alone.

Here again, the *Telegraph Road* is speaking: space and the internet are now moving at the same pace. Yet the present is unevenly distributed. Some are forging ahead to build the future, while others are left pondering the rules as they slide into insignificance.

This is perhaps the first real impact of space exploration: it clarifies the distinction between the builders and the rulers.

### The New Leviathan

The “Telegraph Road” of the 21st century is being paved with silicon and stardust, not asphalt. While the European Union perfects its role as the world’s most expensive concierge—collecting billions in “digital tariffs” through fines while its own space capacity withers to a rounding error—the builders are moving beyond the reach of terrestrial borders.

The merger of SpaceX and xAI is the definitive signal that the “Words vs. Swords” disharmony is being resolved. By placing the “brain” (AI) and the “nervous system” (Starlink) into the same orbital body, Musk is creating a topological high ground where geography is irrelevant and traditional regulation is a friction of the past.

We are entering an era where sovereignty is no longer defined by the lines on a map, but by the altitude of your servers. On this new frontier, the builders are not just creating technology; they are drafting the new code of human civilization. The rulers in Paris and Brussels may keep their summons and their fines, but from 500 kilometers up, the view is clear: The future belongs to those who build the road, not those who wait at the toll booth**.**
