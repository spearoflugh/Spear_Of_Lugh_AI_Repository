# Jason Quixote

Jason Arday is maybe not the Quixotic figure that we deserved but it is the one that we got in this summer 26. It may appear unfair to focus on a particular individual. After all, the real subject here is the state of the institutions. Total decay.

That the guy probably committed suicide is tragic but it doesn’t alleviate the facts. Fighting windmills is the new normal and this affair has become the undeniable proof of it. It is illuminating at many levels.

Don Quixote follows an aging country gentleman who reads so many chivalric romances that he loses his sanity, renames himself Don Quixote de La Mancha, and sets out across Spain with his squire, Sancho Panza, to revive knighthood and right the world’s wrongs. Any resemblance with a social media activist is not coincidental.

Most social media users dream their lives in the palm of their hands. Every instant is recorded and romanticized into Instagram stories. Users exchange likes and attention. The currency of the world of ideas. In this sense we are like modern Quixote. Dreaming to change the world one tweet at a time, accumulating virtue by exchanging on social media and displaying virtue.

Just following the death of Arday we have been able to witness the reactions of the network mob to the story. Arday immediately became the hero, a kind of academic George Floyd. Yet it is the one who denounced his plagiarism, Nathan Cofnas, that was suspended from the university of Ghent. The fact that Cofnas has written a single article on his Substack has not weighed much in this social media trial. The legacy media have published hundreds of articles and the story trended on social media across the world  for days. But somehow all the faults were incumbent upon Cofnas.

As often in digital times things go backwards. In Cervantes novel Don Quixote starts as the dreamer and Sancho Panza is the realist. They slowly exchange their stance. Don Quixote starts to see reality while Sancho becomes intoxicated by the romantic imagination of Quixote. On social media users only enhance their initial character. They are algorithmically encouraged in their way. Just like any discussion with an LLM that always tries to tame its customers through conciliatory exchanges.

The harmony that can be found in Cervantes story has vanished from the virtual world. A complete inversion is to be seen: while aiming high and having feet firmly on the ground could be the moral of Quixote, we observe the opposite of social media crowds. No facts, like the plagiarism proofs of Arday,.would alter their views and no aim is so high that it can be chased in a second by the new trending subject. A strange combination of internal persistence and external absence of orientation. It reminds us that social media is the complete loss of external intimacy—anybody can see your profile and messages—while imposing total internal intimacy —one explicitly chooses what is published and how (filters, AI,.etc )—.

Another similarly ‘heroic’ figure of this 2026 ending summer is Lindsay Clancy. She allegedly killed her three kids by strangulating them in 2023 and the opening of the trial this summer. This horrific affair was also interpreted in a totally surreal way on social media. One number may sum it all: a GoFundMe campaign (to help her parents but transparently about her) reached 1 million dollars in a matter of days. The truckers for freedom were barred from using the platform to support their protest of COVID-19 restrictions. They were accused of blocking the traffic while protesting interdictions by the government to move around, go figure. The same happened to Kyle Rittenhouse who was found not guilty on all counts was also censored from the same platform. Apparently killing three kids makes you a victim on the other hand. The whole situation would be Quixotic if not tragic.

One cannot be but struck by the homomorphic structure of social media reaction in both stories. They reveal that the technology is not as neutral as often presented. The technology shapes our ways of thinking and reacting. Just like Quixote was a reaction to the end of the chivalric era, social media have completely changed the social reception of those affairs. Imagine how both would have been received in the 1990s and you can measure how far the digital revolution has gone.

Another related remark is the reaction of AIs to this article. If I feed the article and ask for comments, I am drowned under procedural remarks and things like “it is controversial you should not use these examples because you should stay in the herd of well behaved people that don’t talk about things that could make someone somewhere uncomfortable”. Well you see what I mean. Surely the subject of a future piece.
