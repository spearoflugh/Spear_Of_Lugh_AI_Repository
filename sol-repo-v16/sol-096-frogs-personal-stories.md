# Frogs Personal Stories

Frogger is one of the most ancient video game. The aim was to make a frog safely crossing a busy highway. It looked like this:

[Embedded video]

The original arcade edition, because at the time people weren’t equipped with computers at home, ran at a vertical refresh rate of approximately 60.61 Hz, which effectively translates to 60 FPS (frames per second). As a measure of comparison Counter Strike 2 can run up to 240-500 FPS depending on how much you are ready to spend in your specialized high-refresh monitors if you are a professional esport player. Competitive gamers require sub-millisecond input response and extreme motion clarity.

This could be just another example of the digital exponential improvement that we are experiencing over the last decades, but the allegory goes deeper than that. The faster and faster succession of news cycle is having deep repercussion in our moral and ethic judgment.

### Ghosting conspiracies

What do Diddy and Hunter Biden have in common? If you’re thinking of lavish lifestyles, cocaine off prostitutes’ butts, and unclear links to the powers that be, think again. Like many other public figures, these indicted characters are being rehabilitated at the speed of light.

It is a direct effect of the accelerated FPS of modern media. News stories come and go so fast that what happened a few months ago—whether it’s running an international human trafficking ring or engaging in high-level corruption—is forgotten after a few podcast appearances and a little PR.

There is a lot going on, after all. It’s hard to keep up with the plague in Russia, wars across the world, the price of diesel, UFO disclosures, maths being solved by AI and so on. On top of that, the rapid succession of these stories makes events from the recent past seem farther away than they really are. Digital culture acts as a rear-view mirror.

We are in a paradoxical situation where an apparently perfect memory—since the internet and digital systems never forget anything—is powerless to help us learn anything from the past. The powers that be have understood this very well.

Just take the case of the war with Iran: there wasn’t even the pretext of a false flag attack, or at least a sustained PR campaign about weapons of mass destruction. No, it was the bare minimum: one press conference by the President followed by 157 contradictory statements—*“the war is over,” “they will sign a deal,” “we will wipe out this civilization,” “we control the Strait of Hormuz,” “the strikes are back on the table.”*

No one, in the institutions, really cares what the people think at this point. Liberal democracy at its best. The final form of “manufacturing consent” has been reached, and much like in *The Communist Manifesto* where the state is eventually supposed to wither away, there is no longer any need to manufacture your consent. They just do things. Anyway if Diddy and Hunter Biden are seen as heroes, then the sky is the limit right?

The paradox is even more pronounced than that: if you submit this text to an AI, it will fact-check everything, telling you that *"1981’s Frogger was ported to the Atari 2600 and Apple II in 1982"* or that Diddy will be freed in 2028... It means that while you have the ability to split hairs into 2¹⁸² pieces and examine each piece with an electron microscope, it is done only to miss the forest for the trees. Like looking at a Monet impressionist painting with a magnifying glass.

### Rabbit holes

If you want a picture of the near future, imagine parallel rabbit holes that don’t interact with each other. Every rabbit is building its own burrow deeper and deeper. With AI and digital technologies, you can go as deep as you like. Last week’s OpenAI release on open mathematical problems even proves that you can go deeper than the best living humans so far. But to go where, and why? The shocked reactions of top-tier mathematicians may give midwits the Schadenfreude of their lives, but the underlying problem remains: why are we doing things? The same question exists for the powers that be, that was what is mentioned in the previous section. As a whole technology has augmented the power of everyone in an unprecedented way. Now what?

Hubris seems to be the most likely explanation if we look at what the powers that be have been doing recently. Maybe for the first time in human history, we can do things before understanding how or why by delegating them to machines. I am talking about both the intent and the actual implementation here. Most targets are chosen by AI nowadays; it is no longer like a gun operated by a human. If a nuke were to be dropped today, what are the odds that the target would be determined by a machine rather than the vacation memories of an American official?

This is directly linked to the speed at which events occur. The faster you go, the less you look around: you are focused on a goal, and the surrounding context becomes mere noise. Yet, what that goal is remains unspecified. The progress of AI and digital technologies is freeing us from the contingencies of the physical world, only to remind us that perfection is not human. You can see the optimization of sport. It is a direct byproduct of digital technologies. Why? Because coaches have ingested data just like AI models do, translating it into actual recommendations for professional players, who—professionals that they are—follow their boss’s instructions. Faster, harder, stronger and duller.

You can find similar examples in many areas: from gonzo porn videos (with no more dialogue or story) to AI-polished social media posts, the world has been filled with shiny objects that you forget as soon as you scroll. The only lingering feeling is one of overall fatigue. How long has it been since you spent a few seconds looking at a plastic bag trapped in a whirlwind?
