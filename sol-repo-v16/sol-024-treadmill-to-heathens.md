# The Treadmill to Heathens

Last week, municipal elections were held in France. In a few towns, LFI (La France Insoumise) won the mayor’s office. I won’t bother you with French internal politics, but this move was representative of a wider phenomenon linked to the shift toward a digital society. Old institutions are simply no longer up to the task. Here, I want to focus on a peculiar institution: language. How is it linked to LFI’s victory? Well, that’s what we are going to explore in this piece.

### ​Migration

​LFI sits at the far left of the political spectrum. I am talking about French politics here, so that actually means something; even libertarians are just another flavor of socialism around here. Lately, they have changed their political standing to become the party of migrants. That is the point. Have you noticed that the words ‘emigration’ and ‘immigration’ (and the linked terms ‘emigrant’ and ‘immigrant’) have disappeared from the journalistic class? The move (pun intended) from an oriented vocabulary—making the distinction between who comes and who leaves—to the more neutral ‘migrant’ is not without consequences for public discussion.

​The lack of a clear distinction between departure and arrival points is the most glaring example showing that orientation has been lost. When I was young, ‘migration’ was the word used to denote the movement of flocks of birds in autumn or spring. Yet even birds know where they are going and why. It is not that the so-called migrants don’t know why they are coming to Western countries—they are escaping hellholes—it is rather that this reasoning has disappeared from public discussion. Why?

​A first answer might be found in the inaugural speech of the freshly elected Mayor of St. Denis. Essentially, the message was that you have to bow to the newcomers because they won the elections. Well, they want to shape their new country like the old one they fled. Even a journalist with the memory of a goldfish would understand that this is not a good omen for St. Denis. So they do what they do best: hide their agenda under litotes. But this is tactical; there are deeper considerations.

### Aether

​As we spend more and more time on digital networks, we move away from the Earth. This is not so much a physical remark, like a SpaceX rocket escaping Earth’s gravity, but rather an observation that on the internet, there is no North or South. This is another aspect of the topological revolution. Unlike birds, social media swarms are oriented by ideas rather than by the Earth’s magnetic field. This shift remains mostly unsaid and unthought; most people feel it but haven’t fully internalized it. The evolution of language that I mentioned at the beginning is a first step.

This, in part, explains why the elites are using such language. Of course, there is an agenda, but there is also a historical force imposing itself upon them. The irony is not lost on anyone when reconsidering Bagayoko’s inaugural speech. He, too, is living in the ‘Cathedral.’ His arguments may sound revolutionary—and they are—but not in the way he thinks. He believes that he, as a representative of the migrant class, is going to be in charge. LFI calls it ‘*La Nouvelle France*‘ — ‘The New France.’ But they don’t understand that the nation-state is a dying concept. It is precisely because it is dying that they exist as a political force.

### Zero-Data identity

Another point is illustrated by this event. The political space is seen as a zero-data identity. The expression is meant to recall zero-day attack in cyber security. A 0-day attack is a cyberattack that targets a software vulnerability that is unknown to the software vendor or the public. Here you have a kind of 0-day attack on the idea of republic. The republic is seen an empty shell and anyone can come and inhabits it. Politics as an hermit crab switching habitat.

The shell has not been hollowed out by the crab; rather, it was hollowed out by digitalization and the technological progress (such as cheap air travel) of our societies. We find ourselves in this uneasy moment where things are unsatisfactory for everyone. These problems are structural and go far beyond topical political discussions regarding the marginal rate of this or that tax. This is why I am exploring alternative ways to understand the issue of living together. The Fibralist proposition is a direct approach to this issue. See you at the Fibralist Corner to talk about that.
