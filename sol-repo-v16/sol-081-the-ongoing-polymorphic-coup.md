# The Ongoing Polymorphic Coup

It's summertime. Where I live, we're currently going through our fourth heatwave of the season—unprecedented, as they say. This piece is going to be unprecedented, too. I originally had the idea in French, and I'm not sure it will make the same sense in English, but I'm too overwhelmed by the heat to resist writing it.

### Coupe-feu (firebreak)

One of the consequences of those heat waves**is** the numerous fires in France. A particularly large one occurred close to Bordeaux. The inefficiency of the authorities to deal with this catastrophe has only been matched by the extraordinary reaction from local communities. In the span of a couple of days, they realized 150 km of deep cuts to circumvent the spread of the fire. Maybe bootstrapping large events from individuals is helped by the information technologies, and it can be seen as a very positive development. Notice though that it is almost always happening in spite of the authorities (some voices were heard that it was not done following rules etc.).

I take it as an allegory of our times in the following sense: reacting has taken over direct action. Of course, no one hopes for a fire to start, and reacting to the blaze in order to limit its consequences is good and natural. But this tendency goes further, led by the flow of information. Being inundated with data creates the reflex to respond to the data. Again, it might appear rational, but there is a blind spot: where has the will gone? The world has been transformed from a place of “life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness” into a long list of multiple choice quizzes. Another name for them is “objective responses”. The former was about an open world, while the latter is about a closed one. The idea of a firebreak is that of an item that is used to break the spread of something. Likewise, multiple choices are hiding the scope of liberty from you. Material ease, together with the ease of gathering information, has slowly but surely turned us into data consumers. We have completely forgotten the role of information producers. Mechanically, the social media environment nudges you into reacting to trends and quoting other posts. I know that this is what a discussion is all about, but there are other ways of communicating. A monologue or a speech before a battle (if it ever happened anywhere other than in history books, because no one after the second rank could hear the voice of the chief) is also a way to communicate.

The firebreaks that I am thinking about are actually freedom breakers. They are used to scatter the will like water in the sand.

### Coupe-circuit (circuit breaker)

In the midst of a never-ending information stream, social media users are mostly self-centered. This translates into having an opinion on the trend *du jour*. The social media crowd became instant ancient Greek enthusiasts after the release of Nolan’s version of the *Odyssey*. A characteristic feature of this movie is the constant introspection and lack of will of the characters. It tells us more about ourselves than about the Greeks a few millennia ago. It is very hard to detect an ounce of motivation behind their moves. Why is Penelope waiting for the return of Odysseus? Even the depiction of Athena is more reminiscent of Jiminy Cricket than of the goddess of reason and strategic warfare. The goddess is seen as an improved version of a shrink.

It will come as no surprise that if most of your energy is focused on yourself, and moreover on your appearance (selfies, filters, etc.), then you are not committed to doing grand things. Another ancient Greek myth is here to help: Narcissus. He was secretly loved by Echo, who had been cursed by Hera. Hera robbed Echo of her voice, leaving her only able to repeat the last few words spoken by others. The parallels with social media are almost too strong to believe. There is another curse in the tale: Nemesis heard the call of another of Narcissus’s lovers who was turned down heartlessly. The prayer was: “May he fall in love with someone else, and may he never possess his love!” Long story short, Narcissus fell in love with his image (and not himself, which is a subtle point to consider) while contemplating his reflection in a lake. The story tells that every time that he touched the surface of the lake, ripples distorted the image. He wept, but his tears disturbed the water, hiding his love from view. Realizing he could never hold or touch the object of his obsession, he was consumed by sorrow and despair. How could we not see here a perfect description of online activism and its relation to reality?

### Coupe-conversation (conversation stopper)

On top of narcissism, which is inherent to the human condition, some tools have been devised to control  narrative freedom on social media. Here again, the phenomenon is not really new, but it has taken on another dimension in digital culture. The idea is to use specific words as boundaries and make further progress in the discussion impossible. From a technical point of view, words are very useful because one can automate the policy around them. Think about what happened during the COVID crisis. But one could multiply the examples. Maybe the most common is “climate change”, which is used as a conversation stopper for any meteorological event: too hot, too humid, too dry, too sunny, etc., is explained by a single concept. The thing is, once this word is uttered, the world is divided into two parts: those who acquiesce and the rest, who are “climate deniers”. By definition, the camp of those adhering to the mono-explanation is more homogeneous than the other, which gathers everyone from authentic flat-earthers to politically engaged people, as well as skeptics who would like to know more.

Another conversation stopper was used this week with the invasion of Ceuta by thousands of illegal migrants. Almost all institutional reactions have mentioned the “migration crisis”. This is because the word “migration” is a conversation stopper. Of course, mostly empty-handed guys stuck in a very small area are not looking for immigration purposes (one has to identify oneself to go from Ceuta to mainland Spain). But the fact that it was a coordinated attack (because immigration laws didn’t change overnight, the last one dating back months to April) is not even discussed. The involvement of the Moroccan authorities, who have at the very least tacitly approved the move, isn’t discussed either. These considerations are hidden behind a single word.

### Coupe-coupe (machete)

Trouble started right after the invasion. The residents of Ceuta were urged to stay home, and some migrants were seen with swords and other improvised weapons. Some stores were looted, and some apartment buildings were attacked. Behind the curtain of the word, reality continued to unfold.

The picture that emerges from all this is paradoxical. The real world is moving, while the virtual world is as if stuck. It is surprising, because there are no physical limits in the realm of ideas. One would have thought that with the advent of AI, it would generate even more thought experiments. Yet the opposite is happening. The real world, with all its inertia, moves far more than the mental world of the Cathedral. Institutions are using new information technologies not to go further, but to entrench their power. As they have lost the battle of legitimacy, they are reduced to the destruction of every form of opposition. In France, we are going to have to prove our age before speaking on social media, starting September 1st. Also, there was a report from the Senate talking about “ingérences intérieures” (*domestic interference*), which sounds a bit weird in a so-called republican democracy… Just having a different opinion from the institutions has become suspicious. It shows that it is no longer the demos that rules. Hence the result, consisting of an ever expanding collection of tools and concepts to “cut” the flow of speech.

I have the intuition that we have reached a key moment. There is this song “L’opportuniste” by Jacques Dutronc which ends with a couplet about political turncoats [lyrics omitted for copyright reasons].
