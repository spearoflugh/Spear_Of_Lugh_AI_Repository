# 377 Reasons Why

Yesterday OpenAI just released a monster batch of new mathematical results against open problems. A weird part of the story being that 372 are numbered 377:

[Embedded X post]

There is far more math to ingest than I could possibly do in what’s left of my life. Amongst the results, the quasi-Riemann hypothesis and Hilbert’s tenth problem (over the rational numbers) are the most salient to me. The latter, mostly because I personally studied the original problem in great detail (in connection with my teaching of computability). But I am not going to talk about those “details” and will rather focus on how it was done and what it might mean for the, very near, future.

### ​GitHub and arXiv

​The first point is not directly linked to AI, but to the evolution of academic publishing that it underlies. I have written a lot about the fact that digital culture was going to topple current peer-review-based academia. A first sign was the multiplication of publications on arXiv. At the beginning of September 2026, another sign was clear: the 60k ICLR submissions. A kind of DDoS attack against peer review has been reached, which is, of course, a by-product of AI progress. Yesterday was a new landmark: the batch is so huge that the 37? release—replacing the question mark with 7 or 2 is not yet clear—has been done on GitHub. Just like a student project would be.

​It may look superficial, but it is very revealing of the status of academic papers today. What was the pinnacle of an academic career has turned into a basic commodity. Overnight. Just like when Cinderella sees her gala dress turn back into rags when the clock strikes midnight.

### The pumpkin coach

​Is AI the sorcerer that turned the pumpkin into a coach? The allegory seems to fit, just like the glass slipper to Cinderella’s foot. After all, she remained the love of the prince despite appearances and etiquette. In the fairy tale, love at first sight always wins the day. Will the love of math do as well?

​This is where true mathematicians, the ones doing it for the love of the game, will be revealed. Even covered in rags, they will not experience a change of heart toward the discipline. The question is: what is the glass slipper in this silicon-boosted story? Does it exist?

Another point of the tale is that the coach goes back to its original form. It turns back into a pumpkin, just like the butlers to rats. The magic spell only lasts so long. Surely OpenAI will stop actively doing fundamental maths after having showcased what their system was able to do. Not without remembering what IBM did just after having defeated Kasparov with Deep Blue: they just dismantled the supercomputer and benefited from the spike in the stock market, temporary because everything is just borrowed down here.
