# The Battlenetwork of the Present

Since last week, France has been the subject of high school riots. Many high schools have been burned; and while human casualties are low, the scale of the unrest is significant—even for a country where this activity could be considered a national sport. Here is a video of the Bordeaux skyline last Thursday; it looks like Kyiv after a night of drone attacks:

[Embedded X post]

This hot news environment made me wondered about the possibility of successful riots in a digital culture.

### What do you mean by “successful”?

I am not really thinking about those seasonal riots. My point is broader: as the digital revolution unfolds, old institutions will be toppled and replaced by new ones—much like what the French Revolution did for monarchies and empires. A pair of maps is worth a thousand words: monarchies are in red and republics are in blue (1788 on the left, today on the right):

It took a century and more, and was not a smooth process, but the transition is almost complete. Moreover, Sweden and Norway are effectively republics with a figurehead king, and in the other countries, royal power is usually limited by strict constitutions—a far cry from what medieval kings were.

This is my definition of success in this piece. Note that it goes a step further than the usual 'color revolutions', because they tend only to change who is in charge. I am thinking of transformations that will change *what it means to be in charge.*

### Organized force in digital times

If recent history serves as a guide, things will move much faster for the next generation of institutions to dominate the landscape. Or not. Because it is not like old institutions won't fight tooth and nail to keep control. Hence the question: what could topple them? Digital technologies enhance the power of everyone: institutions and their enemies alike. So the question is rather: who is going to use digital technologies the best, and how, to successfully implement their will?

The first thing to note is that you can forget about revolutionary romanticism. The power of modern weaponry defies imagination. Throwing a Molotov cocktail or using a shotgun won't cut it against a truly organized force. Again, visuals speak louder than words:

[Embedded X post]

This is presumably a video of an exercise (because the cameraman appears very calm), but it gives you a hint of what could happen if the powers that be take off the gloves. Rioters don’t stand a better chance of defeating an organized force than a toddler has at knocking out Mike Tyson at his peak.

One could retort that digital technologies can be used to synchronize individuals, making their actions more efficient. However, this is true only to an extent. First, it assumes that the digital infrastructure will remain operational during the revolution. When you see that it only takes a few kids to push the powers that be to consider blocking social networks, you understand that it is far from a given. Second, it requires an enormous amount of effort to stop a professional army from controlling the land—so much so that, in fact, success would mean that a supermajority of the population is dead set against the powers that be. This points to the true nature of the fight: if a supermajority is already in the opposition, it means that legitimacy is about to shift. And it reveals the true goal of an insurrection in the digital age: the aim is not to defeat the military troops, which is impossible, but to make them fight for you.

### Battlenetwork

In a civil war, when the fight happens on the battlefield, it has already been decided in the battlenetwork. The faction that has successfully imposed its narrative is going to win. You now understand why the powers that be are multiplying laws and regulations regarding misinformation, disinformation, and malinformation.

The battlenetwork is not just the information space. The ability to synchronize is what is paramount. More than just the sharing of information, the organizational possibilities offered by a network are far more powerful. Controlling the virality of a message is the new censorship. Just slowing down its circulation might be enough to nip a nascent social movement in the bud.

Hence, the powers that be may no longer be what you think they are. Of course, old institutions still run the police and the army, yet they rely on tech corporations for help. Right now, the situation is a kind of win-win: these companies receive preferential legal treatment in exchange for letting states interfere on their turf. But it is not a stable equilibrium. Day by day, we can see the power of tech rising—from AI and the space industry to power production (for data centers)—while the power of established institutions is declining. The legitimacy of those institutions is plummeting all around the globe.

Maybe you have noticed someone absent from the piece. Where are the people? Viewed primarily as taxpayers by states and data providers by tech, ordinary people seemingly have disappeared from the political landscape. People feel it, and it explains the feeling of insurgency all across the globe. Power is drifting away from the public. You remember what 'republic' means, right?

### Leap of Praxis

I am now shifting the level of discourse. Here is what I think could work. It is just a waking dream at this point—maybe a bit more, something like an educated guess. Tech will hollow out the powers that be: remember the DOGE experiment? They cut internet to some agencies and blocked their access to the network. The agencies crumbled from within: they could file lawsuits and whatnot, but due to the slow pace of the judiciary, they were wiped off the map before anything concrete happened. Tech can use the inertia of the bureaucracy against it, like judo.

​But where are the people in this story? The last paragraph is clear: the point is not to take over a dying bureaucracy. That is Gramsci, something from the last century. From another planet. It is time to update ourselves, folks: take control of the tech from the inside. You think it sounds crazy? Then what are the Effective Altruist guys doing? They might be insufferable, but they are not stupid.
