  <key_concepts>
    <concept name="Duality vs Symmetry — The Mathematical Foundation of Social Inversions">
      The article introduces a precise mathematical distinction that is the implicit
      analytical backbone of the entire corpus but never again formalized explicitly.
      Symmetry is involutive: the symmetrical of the symmetrical recovers the original.
      Duality is NOT necessarily involutive: the dual of the dual is not always the primal.
      Applied to social phenomena, this distinction explains why apparent "inversions" do
      not cancel each other and why two wrongs do not make a right in any simple arithmetic
      sense. Examples: Antifa as coFascism (same objects, arrows reversed — violence as
      means, purity as goal — yet producing indistinguishable street behavior), Wokeism as
      coChristianity (same ritual structures — kneeling, foot-washing — with source and
      target reversed), mob justice as co-justice (produced in the name of justice,
      structurally identical to the lynching it opposes). The formal tool used is category
      theory: a cocategory has the same objects but morphisms with reversed direction.
      Objects remain constant; only the direction of relations changes. This is why
      duality produces similarity without identity — and why it is more dangerous than
      simple opposition.
    </concept>
    <concept name="Co- Prefix as Analytical Tool">
      The article proposes the prefix "co-" (from category theory) to replace "anti-"
      or "inverse-" when describing phenomena that are structurally related but not
      simply opposed. coFascism (Antifa), coChristianity (Wokeism), coJustice (mob rule
      in courts), co-N-word (racialized "white" epithets). The prefix signals: same objects,
      reversed morphisms, not necessarily self-inverse under double application. This is
      the formal grounding for the whole corpus pattern of "inversion without cancellation"
      that appears in: sol-013 (Homeopathogenesis: accuse of what you do), sol-013c
      (Dermatophobia: disgust deployed against disgust), sol-011c (Conflaxion: inclusion
      becoming the new exclusion), sol-015b (Mobius strip: alternation without change).
      Without the co- framework, these patterns appear as contradictions. With it,
      they are coherent dualities.
    </concept>
    <concept name="Semi-Decidability and the Limits of Intuition">
      The article uses semi-decidability from computability theory as a formal model for
      why intuition fails with dualities. A semi-decidable problem has a yes-answer that
      eventually halts, but a no-answer that may run forever. Crucially, the co-problem of
      a semi-decidable problem is NOT semi-decidable — a result that violates naive intuition
      (since the co-problem of a decidable problem IS decidable). This formal result models
      the social situation: when dealing with dualities, you cannot simply "flip" your
      conclusions. The co of a good conclusion is not simply a bad conclusion — it may be
      something qualitatively different that no intuitive shortcut reaches. Applied to the
      corpus: the Topological Revolution (sol-002) is not simply "reversed" by going back
      to physical communities; the dual of inclusion authoritarianism (sol-011c) is not
      simply freedom; the co-Katechon (sol-033) is not simply chaos.
    </concept>

<content>
This article develops the mathematical concept of duality — distinct from symmetry — as the
key analytical tool for understanding social and political inversions in the digital era.
Using examples from category theory and computability theory, the author argues that apparent
paradoxes (Antifa acting like fascists, mob justice produced in the name of justice, wokeist
rituals mirroring Christian ones) are not logical contradictions but coherent dualities where
objects remain constant and morphisms reverse direction.

Key formal argument: the co-problem of a semi-decidable problem is NOT semi-decidable —
a result that violates naive intuition and models why social dualities cannot be resolved
by simply "flipping" the analysis. Two applications of a duality do not recover the original.

Applied taxonomy of 2020 social inversions analyzed as co-phenomena:
- coChristianity (Wokeism): kneeling at the perpetrator's position; foot-washing with
  direction reversed (officials washing activists' feet vs. Jesus washing apostles' feet)
- coFascism (Antifa): same objects (violence, purity enforcement), morphisms reversed
  (direction of who excludes whom) — produces indistinguishable street behavior
- coJustice (mob rule in courts): riots in the name of justice create conditions where
  due process is impossible, structurally replicating the lynching they oppose
- co-N-word (racialized "white" epithets): purity/impurity inversion along the same
  racial axis with reversed target

The author proposes the prefix "co-" to replace "anti-" in political analysis: coFascist
rather than Antifa, because "anti-" wrongly implies a simple negation that cancels on
double application, while "co-" correctly signals a structural relationship that is not
necessarily self-inverse.

Methodological conclusion: "We should always be aware of the limitations of the language.
Opposition is not reducible to classical logical negation and what, on the surface, appear
as raw incoherence may in fact be the witness of subtle dualities."
